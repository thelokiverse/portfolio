02  —  What I pay attention to

I don’t start with
the solution.

I start with the problem, figure out what actually matters, and work backwards from there.

01
Understand the problem.
Before getting into features, I want to understand who has the problem, how they deal with it today, and what would actually make their life better.
+
02
Make the trade-off.
There’s usually more worth building than there is time to build it. I like making the hard calls explicit — what we’re doing, what we’re not, and why.
+
03
Let the product teach you.
Shipping changes the conversation. Once something is in people’s hands, their behaviour tells you what was right, what wasn’t, and what to do next. I’ve shipped features where the biggest lever turned out to be something we didn’t plan for — like discovering that an AI product’s real bottleneck was discovery, not quality.
+
03  —  Selected work

Things I’ve shipped.

I can’t show you the products. I can show you the problems, decisions, and what happened after we shipped.
01
Taking an AI product from 0 → 1
AI 0 → 1 Product
+
We had years of proprietary market data — company profiles, funding rounds, sector maps. The problem was that getting an answer still meant knowing exactly where to look. The hard part wasn’t building the LLM layer. It was deciding what the assistant should be trusted to answer versus what it shouldn’t touch, and then realising the real adoption bottleneck wasn’t quality — it was discovery. Nobody was finding the thing. Adding entry points on list pages and the home dashboard is what actually moved the curve.
02
Building AI workflows people actually use
AI Workflow Adoption
+
I kept watching PMs write the same types of requirements from scratch — competitive analyses, feature specs, prioritisation frameworks. Same structure, different inputs. I turned those into AI-assisted workflows, but the interesting part was what happened after: some stuck immediately, others nobody touched. The ones that worked handled the tedious middle of the task — PMs wanted to define the problem themselves and review the output themselves, they just didn’t want to do the assembly in between. Nearly the entire PM team adopted them.
03
Owning the first five minutes
Product Activation
+
The first-user experience had to serve investors scouting deals, corporates tracking competitors, and sales teams building prospect lists — all in the same flow. The Lite product was trying to do too much for everyone and landing for nobody. I owned the decision of what a new user should see first, which meant deciding who the Lite experience was actually for and what “value” looked like in the first session before asking them to pay.
04
Growing without diluting lead quality
Growth Acquisition
+
Acquisition was scaling — signups went from dozens a day to hundreds. But more users isn’t the same as more of the right users. I reoriented content and landing pages toward specific customer segments that converted at higher value, instead of optimising for raw volume. The hard part was making the case that slowing down on total signups to improve mix was the right trade.
